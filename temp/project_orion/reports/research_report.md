## 1. Abstract

This research investigation evaluates whether an adaptive, risk-constrained graph clustering algorithm can achieve superior coverage at fixed low contamination levels compared to conventional baselines (Thresholded Connected Components, Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering, and Reciprocal Nearest-Neighbor Graph Clustering) on anonymous 192-dimensional embeddings. The problem setting assumes that false merges (contamination) carry a substantially higher penalty than false splits (fragmentation), requiring algorithms to support conservative merging and explicit abstention. Across 60 held-out evaluation runs spanning 3 independent random seeds (`55, 99, 2048`) and 5 outlier contamination rates (`0.01` to `0.50`), our proposed `RiskGraphClusterer` achieved a flawless **0.0000 cluster contamination rate** and **1.0000 pairwise precision**, completely preventing any cross-entity merge errors. However, because of compound conservative filters (reciprocal consistency, local density calibration, cohesion validation, and abstention thresholds), the method suffered severe cluster fragmentation, achieving an average Pairwise Recall of **0.0612** and Coverage of **0.0000** (Adjusted Rand Index: **0.1048**). In comparison, the simple `Thresholded Graph` baseline achieved zero contamination (`0.0000`) while maintaining an ARI of **0.7909**, Pairwise Recall of **0.7164**, and Coverage of **0.1333**. Consequently, **Hypothesis H1 is REJECTED by empirical evidence**. We document the mathematical mechanics of this failure, present ablation and sensitivity sweeps, benchmark scalability up to 200,000 embeddings, and outline future directions for adaptive multi-stage cluster recovery.

## 2. Problem Formulation & Research Question

### 2.1 Formal Formulation
Given a set of $N$ high-dimensional embedding vectors:
$$X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_N\}, \quad x_i \in \mathbb{R}^{192}, \quad \|x_i\|_2 = 1$$
generated by an unknown number of latent entities $K$, where each entity $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ generates multiple observations subject to intra-cluster variance, domain shifts, and noise. The clustering algorithm maps each observation $x_i$ to a cluster label $c_i \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{-1\}$, where $c_i = -1$ denotes explicit abstention.

The objective is to discover entity groupings while strictly bounding the **False Match Rate (FMR)** and **Cluster Contamination**:
$$\text{FMR} = \frac{\sum_{i < j} \mathbb{I}(c_i = c_j \land y_i \neq y_j)}{\sum_{i < j} \mathbb{I}(c_i = c_j)}$$
$$\text{Contamination}(C) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c \in C} \mathbb{I}(|\{y_i : c_i = c\}| > 1)$$
where false merges are prioritized over false splits.

### 2.2 Research Question and Hypothesis
- **Research Question**: Can an adaptive, risk-constrained graph-clustering algorithm achieve a better contamination-versus-coverage tradeoff than conventional clustering baselines?
- **Hypothesis H1**: Risk-constrained graph clustering can achieve higher coverage at a fixed, low contamination level than conventional graph-clustering baselines.

## 3. Related Methodological Background

1. **Threshold-based Connected Components**: Connects nodes if $S_{ij} = \langle x_i, x_j \rangle \ge \tau$. Sensitive to single "bridge" points that cause catastrophic transitive chaining and high contamination.
2. **Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering (Ward/Average)**: Iteratively merges clusters minimizing variance or average distance. Prone to boundary distortion under unequal cluster sizes or noisy outliers.
3. **Reciprocal Nearest-Neighbor Graphs (RNNG)**: Requires mutual nearest-neighbor status ($j \in \text{kNN}(i) \land i \in \text{kNN}(j)$) to form edges.
4. **Local Density Calibration & Conformal Risk Control**: Normalizing similarities by local neighborhood density $\mu_i$ suppresses hubness and spurious high-dimensional similarities.

## 4. Algorithmic Description: RiskGraphClusterer

The proposed method (`src/orion/risk_graph.py`, lines 39–175) combines five protective mechanisms before permitting any two nodes or micro-clusters to merge:

1. **Adaptive Neighborhood Candidate Selection**: For node $i$, retrieve top-$K$ candidates based on cosine similarity:
   $$\mathcal{N}_K(i) = \text{argtop}_K (S_{i,:})$$
2. **Reciprocal Neighborhood Consistency**: Candidate $j \in \mathcal{N}_K(i)$ is considered only if:
   $$S_{ij} \ge \tau_{\text{recip}} \quad \text{and} \quad S_{ji} \ge \tau_{\text{recip}}$$
3. **Local Density Calibration**: Edge $(i, j)$ requires similarity to exceed the local background density:
   $$S_{ij} > \alpha_{\text{calib}} \cdot \bar{S}_i, \quad \text{where } \bar{S}_i = \frac{1}{|\{k : S_{ik} > 0\}|} \sum_{k: S_{ik} > 0} S_{ik}$$
4. **Cluster Cohesion Constraint**: Any proposed cluster $C$ must satisfy internal mutual density:
   $$\frac{1}{|C|(|C|-1)} \sum_{u, v \in C, u \neq v} S_{uv} \ge \tau_{\text{cohesion}}$$
5. **Confidence-Based Abstention**: If maximum candidate similarity is below $\tau_{\text{abstain}}$, the node is assigned to its own isolated cluster (or abstained).

## 11. Failure Analysis & Research Categorization

In compliance with reporting standards, claims are categorized as follows:

### 11.1 Confirmed Facts
1. All 60 evaluation runs (`artifacts/evaluation/results.json`) completed deterministically.
2. `risk_aware` produced zero false merges across all evaluation seeds and contamination rates.
3. `threshold_graph` outperformed `risk_aware` in recall and coverage at zero contamination.

### 11.2 Inferences & Algorithmic Diagnosis
The failure of `risk_aware` to achieve high coverage is attributable to **compound conservative filtering**:
When $x_i$ and $x_j$ are subject to intra-cluster noise $\sigma_{\text{noise}} = 0.05$ and domain shifts $\sigma_{\text{domain}}$, their cosine similarity drops below 0.80. Even if $S_{ij} \ge 0.70$, the requirement that $S_{ij} > \alpha \cdot \bar{S}_i$ (local calibration) filters out valid matches in dense regions. The graph becomes severely disconnected, causing valid clusters to shatter into singletons.

### 11.3 Limitations & Synthetic Assumptions
1. **Synthetic Embedding Assumptions**: As specified in `docs/architecture.md` and `src/orion/data.py`, vectors are drawn from Gaussian distributions on the unit sphere with synthetic domain shifts. These properties have not been validated on real-world acoustic or speaker embeddings.
2. **Runtime Dependencies**: Memory profiling results rely on local OS virtual memory metrics (`psutil`/`memory_profiler`) on Linux 6.8.0.

### 11.4 Open Questions for Future Research
1. Can multi-hop transitivity recovery (e.g., path-based minimum cut confidence) reconnect fragmented clusters without introducing bridge contamination?
2. Would learned adaptive edge thresholds (via self-supervised contrastive calibration) outperform static geometric heuristics?
